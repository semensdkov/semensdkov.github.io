Discover the ancient art of saponification — a time-tested chemical process that transforms used cooking oil into powerful, biodegradable detergent. No harsh chemicals, no waste, just nature's cleaning solution.

What is Saponification?

Saponification is a chemical reaction that has been used for thousands of years to make soap. The word comes from the Latin "sapo," meaning soap.

When fats or oils react with an alkaline solution (like lye), they undergo a transformation. The triglyceride molecules in the oil break apart and recombine with the alkali to form two things:

  • Soap molecules — the cleaning agents that lift dirt and grease
  • Glycerin — a natural moisturizer that makes soap gentle

At Aurelle, we've perfected this process using your used cooking oil, creating a premium detergent that's tough on grime but kind to the planet.

The Chemistry Behind It

A closer look at the molecular transformation

Triglycerides

Cooking oil is made of triglyceride molecules — three fatty acid chains attached to a glycerol backbone. These are the building blocks of our soap.

Alkaline Solution

We use potassium hydroxide (KOH), commonly known as lye. This strong base breaks the chemical bonds in the triglycerides.

The Reaction

When oil meets lye, the triglycerides split apart. The fatty acids bond with sodium to form soap molecules, while glycerol becomes free glycerin.

Soap Molecules

Each soap molecule has a water-loving head and an oil-loving tail. This dual nature allows soap to lift grease and dirt so water can wash them away.
